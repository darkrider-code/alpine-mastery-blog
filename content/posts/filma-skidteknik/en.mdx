**To analyse your ski technique, use a video that shows the whole skier, both skis and several consecutive turns.** A phone is a good starting point. Clear images and camera placement matter more than the length of the clip.

This guide covers alpine skiing on a piste. It includes a filming checklist, a way to review your own skiing and a next step if you want personal feedback.

## How to film skiing for video analysis

### 1. Choose your location before the run

Ask a friend to stop in a visible, safe place beside the piste. Film where the slope is easy to see and there is room to keep clear of other skiers. Avoid blind crests and narrow sections. Ski normally within your ability; you do not need to increase your speed for the camera.

Agree in advance where the skier will pass. The person filming should remain stationary and follow the run without stepping into traffic on the piste.

### 2. Start at an angle from the front and follow the skier past you

An oblique view from the front can help show both the skis and upper body through several turns. Continue filming as the skier passes to include a side view. No angle shows everything: footage from directly behind offers different observations from a side view.

Keep the skier large enough in the frame to distinguish both skis. If the skier is a tiny dot, high resolution rarely helps. Avoid strong digital zoom and rapid camera movements.

### 3. Keep the whole skier and both skis in view

The head, hips, legs and both skis need to be visible together. Leave some space above and in front of the skier. Landscape orientation can provide room to follow the turns, but choose whichever orientation actually keeps the skier in frame.

Record at normal speed and save the original file. Use slow playback during your review. A heavily cropped or edited clip with frequent cuts provides less context.

### 4. Capture several turns in a short clip

Aim for about 10–20 seconds with several consecutive turns, if the location and run allow it. This is a practical guideline, not a technical minimum. A clear short clip is more useful than a long video in which the skier is barely visible.

Check the current file format, file size and duration limits on the upload page. There is no need to use the maximum permitted clip length.

## Checklist before uploading

- Can you follow the same skier through the entire sequence?
- Are both skis and the whole body visible through several turns?
- Are there left and right turns to compare?
- Have you saved the original without changing playback speed?
- Do you know what you were trying to do and what you want to understand?

When filming your child, explain how the video will be used and choose a clip they are comfortable with. Avoid close-up footage of other skiers.

## Analyse your skiing video in three steps

**Watch the whole run at normal speed.** Start with rhythm, turn shape and control. Look for recurring patterns; do not assume a single frame describes the entire technique.

**Review one question in slow motion.** For example: do the skis begin skidding at turn initiation or only near the end? Does it happen in both directions? Compare several turns on similar terrain.

**Choose one focus for your next run.** Note the observation and ask a ski instructor, coach or video analysis service to help turn it into a drill suited to your level. Film again under similar conditions to see what changes.

## What can a phone video show?

Clear footage can support feedback on visible movement patterns, turn shape and timing. It does not directly measure force against the snow or pressure under the skis. Camera perspective also means an apparent angle is not necessarily a precisely measured edge angle.

## Common questions about filming ski technique

### Do I need an action camera?

No. Phone footage recorded by someone standing beside the piste can be sufficient. A helmet camera showing only the slope ahead does not provide an external view of your body and skis.

### Can I use an old skiing video?

Yes, if the skier and skis are clearly visible. It shows how you skied then. Record new footage when you want to follow your current development.

### Can I film a child training slalom?

Yes. Follow the club's rules and agree a suitable location with the coach. Ask for one technical focus before filming. See our [parent's guide to children's ski technique](/en/skidteknik-barn).
