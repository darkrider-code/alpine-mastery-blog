Knee injuries can have serious consequences for a skier. Research into injury situations helps explain how loads arise, but an ordinary skiing video cannot determine whether a particular skier will injure their anterior cruciate ligament (ACL).

## What does slip-catch mean?

Bere and colleagues analysed 20 ACL injuries among World Cup ski racers. One mechanism they described was slip-catch: the skier loses grip on the outside ski, which suddenly catches an edge again. In the cases analysed, this could involve internal rotation and valgus loading of the knee. The study also described dynamic snowplough and back-weighted landing mechanisms.

These are descriptions of actual injuries in elite racing. They do not show that a particular leg position in a recreational skier's phone video predicts a future injury.

Source: [Bere et al. (2011), Mechanisms of anterior cruciate ligament injury in World Cup alpine skiing: a systematic video analysis of 20 cases](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21515807/).

## What can you discuss with your coach?

Video can support a conversation about recurring loss of balance, turn control and differences between left and right. Begin with what is actually visible and what you experienced during the run.

- When in the turn did you lose control or grip?
- Does the same event recur under similar conditions?
- Are the speed, terrain and task appropriate to your current level?
- Should the training task or the day's workload change?

Camera angle, snow and hidden body parts affect interpretation. A visual movement observation is not a clinical diagnosis or a direct measurement of knee joint loading.

## Preparation needs to suit the individual

Discuss strength, balance, coordination and recovery with a coach or physiotherapist who understands your level and any previous injuries. Choose exercises and progression appropriate to your circumstances. A general blog article cannot decide which programme is suitable for an individual knee.

Stop skiing after a suspected injury and seek medical assessment. Do not use an analysis report to decide whether to keep skiing with pain or instability.

## Returning after an ACL injury

Return to skiing and competition needs an individual plan with the professionals treating you. A certain number of months, a single test result or a positive video analysis is not, by itself, clearance to return.

Ask your rehabilitation professional about the relevant terrain, speed and type of training. Distinguish being able to take part in an activity from being ready for your previous competition workload.
