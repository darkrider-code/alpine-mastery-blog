**The most useful support is often helping your child understand one thing at a time.** Ask what they want to practise, let the coach help choose an appropriate focus and use a short video to discuss what happens during the run.

This guide covers alpine skiing and slalom. It is for parents who want to understand feedback at the club or help their child reflect after ski school.

## Start with your child's experience

First ask: “What felt good?” and “What were you trying to do?” Then watch the video together if your child wants to. The images may differ from how the run felt, but those feelings remain useful information.

Keep the conversation short. A tired or disappointed child does not need a technical review immediately after every run. Choose a moment when they are curious and want to watch.

## Understand the coach's technical terms

### Balance and position

Position includes how your child stands and moves over the skis. It changes through a turn; it is not one pose to hold throughout the run. Ask the coach to demonstrate what your child should feel or try.

### The outside ski

The outside ski is the ski on the outside of the current turn. In a left turn, the right ski is the outside ski. The skis exchange roles in the next turn. Understanding the term makes it easier to discuss the coach's instructions without adding your own.

### Edging, carving and skidding

Edging means tilting the ski against the snow. In carving, the skis mainly follow their edges; a skidded turn also includes sideways movement. Skidding is useful for steering and controlling speed. Clean carved turns are not the right goal in every situation.

Read more about [carving technique and reviewing turns](/en/biomekaniken-bakom-svangen).

### Timing and rhythm

Timing describes when a movement happens. Rhythm describes how turns follow one another. Ask which part of the turn the coach means: initiation, the middle or the transition to the next turn. This gives you a clearer question for the video.

## Use video without becoming an extra coach

Agree with your child's coach what to film and which question the video should help answer. If the coach is working on a specific focus, follow that rather than introducing several new instructions.

1. Film consecutive turns with the whole child and both skis visible.
2. Let your child explain what they were trying to do before offering feedback.
3. Look together for a recurring pattern rather than one isolated “mistake”.
4. Take a concrete question to the coach, such as: “Is there a difference between left and right here?”
5. Follow up the same focus at a later session if your child wants to.

Use our [filming guide with camera angles and a checklist](/en/filma-skidteknik).

## Compare your child with their own earlier skiing

Use similar terrain and a similar task when comparing videos. Speed, snow and fatigue can affect a run. A clip looking different does not automatically mean the technique has improved or deteriorated.

A useful note might be: “Today I kept the same rhythm through the whole section.” Avoid making another skier's style or an analysis score the goal of every session.

## Common questions from parents

### My child still uses a snowplough. Should we practise carving?

Start from your child's current level and ask their instructor about the next step. They need to be able to stop, steer and control speed on the current slope before increasing difficulty. A carving guide is not a plan every child should follow at the same age.

### How often should we film?

There is no requirement to film every session. Record when you have a question or want to follow up a focus. Let your child's enjoyment and willingness guide how much time you spend reviewing footage.

### Should I repeat the instructions from the AI analysis?

First check what the analysis refers to in the video. If it conflicts with the coach's instructions, discuss the difference before changing focus. Automated interpretations can miss context.
